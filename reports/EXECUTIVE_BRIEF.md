# Queensland road-safety investment prioritisation

## Decision brief

**Recommendation:** use two complementary prioritisation tracks. Direct high-capacity program planning toward LGAs carrying the largest absolute fatal-and-serious-injury burden, while commissioning targeted diagnostic reviews in regional LGAs with high resident-normalised burden. Do not collapse the two questions into one score.

Analysis period: 2020-2024, the five latest complete calendar years in the current release.

## What the evidence shows

### 1. Queensland's recorded fatal-and-serious-injury burden increased

Fatalities plus hospitalisations increased from 7,293 in 2020 to 8,943 in 2024, a 22.6% increase. Reported crashes increased by 18.2% over the same endpoints. Fatal-and-serious casualties per 100 reported crashes rose from 60.04 to 62.29.

This is a comparison of two endpoints, not an estimate of a causal trend. Pandemic-related travel changes and reporting conditions must be considered when interpreting 2020.

### 2. Metropolitan and large regional councils carry the largest absolute burden

Across 2020-2024, Brisbane recorded 7,609 fatal-and-serious casualties, followed by Gold Coast (4,079), Logan (3,329), Moreton Bay (3,278) and Sunshine Coast (2,180). All five remained in the highest LGA burden quintile in every year examined.

These locations are logical candidates for scalable programs because they represent the largest volume of harm. Their rankings should not be interpreted as resident-level road-risk rankings.

### 3. Population normalisation changes the regional picture

Among LGAs with at least 100 fatal-and-serious casualties during the five-year period, the largest resident-normalised burdens included Cook, Somerset, Isaac, Charters Towers and North Burnett. Brisbane's absolute burden was the largest, but its resident-normalised measure was substantially lower.

Population is not traffic exposure. Tourist travel, freight, road kilometres, vehicle kilometres travelled and through-traffic can materially affect an LGA's crash burden. These rates therefore identify places for further investigation; they are not comparative probabilities that a resident will crash.

### 4. Recorded factor profiles differ across police regions

For 2020-2024 published aggregate records, Far Northern had the highest recorded drink-driving share (11.81%). Central showed relatively elevated drink-driving (9.77%), speed (5.15%) and fatigue (4.63%) shares. Brisbane had lower corresponding shares in this source profile.

Recorded involvement is not proof of causation, and enforcement/reporting differences may affect observed shares.

## Recommended actions

### A. Establish separate burden and diagnostic funding streams

- Use absolute fatal-and-serious casualties and persistence to identify high-volume program areas.
- Use resident-normalised burden, minimum-count thresholds and uncertainty warnings to select regional diagnostic reviews.
- Do not publish a single composite “risk score” that hides the trade-off between volume and rate.

### B. Commission corridor-level investigation before selecting treatments

For each priority LGA, combine this portfolio analysis with traffic exposure, road geometry, speed surveys, enforcement information and treatment history. Move from LGA screening to corridor/intersection diagnosis before recommending infrastructure or enforcement measures.

### C. Tailor diagnostic questions to regional profiles

- In Far Northern and Central police regions, investigate the contexts behind higher recorded alcohol, speed and fatigue shares.
- In large metropolitan LGAs, focus on scalable interventions where the absolute preventable burden is largest.
- Track vulnerable road-user involvement separately from general vehicle involvement.

## Limitations that affect the decision

- 2025 is incomplete and preliminary and is excluded from like-for-like annual rankings.
- The analysis is observational and cannot establish causal effects.
- ABS population is a resident denominator, not traffic exposure.
- LGA boundaries use current ABS geography for historical population estimates; local boundary changes require review.
- The five supplementary TMR files are published aggregates and cannot be joined to individual crashes.
- Severity weights are transparent scenario values for prioritisation only, not monetary valuations or a validated index.

## Next evidence required

1. Vehicle kilometres travelled or traffic-count exposure by corridor/LGA.
2. Road length and road-class mix.
3. Existing and completed treatment locations and dates.
4. Enforcement activity and detection data.
5. Confidence intervals and sensitivity analysis for regional rate comparisons.
